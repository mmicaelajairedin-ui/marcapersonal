CV and ATS filters

You get filtered out before anyone reads you.

Most applications aren't rejected by a person: they're filtered out by software. Understanding how that filter works is the difference between your CV reaching a desk or never getting there.

Home · CV & ATS

What an ATS is, and why it affects you

An ATS (Applicant Tracking System) is the software companies use to receive and sort applications. The most common ones are Workday, Greenhouse and Lever. Before a recruiter opens your CV, the system has already read it, turned it into text and ranked it.

That's where a lot of good applications get lost. Not for lack of experience, but because the CV is written for a human to read and not to survive that intermediate step.

What I see most often

A headline that doesn't say what you're aiming for

If your CV leads with your current job title instead of the role you want, the system files you where you came from, not where you're going.

Responsibilities instead of achievements

"Responsible for team management" says nothing. "Led a team of five and cut turnover by 20%" does. A verb, what you did, and the number when there is one.

Formats the system can't read

Columns, tables, icons, your name inside a header image. It looks good on screen and arrives at the other end as a jumble.

One CV for every job

The same document sent to thirty different roles fits none of them well. You don't have to rewrite it: adjusting the headline and the first few bullets to each posting is enough.
